2 · Understanding Emerson in his historical context (1690–1841)

New England held on to Locke, through the founders and Unitarian Harvard, long after Europe had started arguing past him with Kant and the Romantics. Coleridge’s Aids to Reflection crossed the Atlantic in 1829 and gave Emerson the tool to break out: Reason (intuition) above Understanding (logic). The irony: he used European ideas to declare America’s intellectual independence from Europe. Columns are periods, not to scale.

3 · From the Enlightenment on: how philosophy split into two streams

One Atlantic tradition until about 1780. Then Europe moves past Locke (Kant, Romanticism) while New England keeps him. Emerson briefly pulls the streams together by importing Romanticism; after that, history drives them apart: the Civil War and industrial boom push America toward the practical, the World Wars push Europe toward crisis and meaning. In the 21st century they meet again. Columns are eras, not to scale.

4 · From Emerson to Nietzsche, from Nietzsche to the modern day

Emerson was one of five sources Nietzsche drew on. Nietzsche never wore the existentialist label, but he became the forefather of existentialism, depth psychology and postmodernism, was hijacked by fascism and later rescued. The 21st century still lives in the rubble of his “wrecking ball”, and Rorty and Han offer two ways forward. Open as its own page.
